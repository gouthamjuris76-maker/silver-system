How Indian investors would price this round

Pre-money by the runway-and-stake rule
Angels needed at this cheque size

  • Angels have no formula. Sarthak Ahuja: angels ask for a stake that feels worth their cheque and want 2–5× in 4–5 years, by selling to a later VC. VCs fund the next 18–24 months of runway for 15–20% and want 15–20× in 5 years, 8 at most. They compare you to competitors on revenue multiples. Private equity and DCF come much later.
  • Dilution adds up. Ahuja's thumb rule is about 17.5% a round on average, 20–25% in early rounds, over four rounds.
  • Simple rules angels use. Tarun Mahajan: before revenue, a five-point checklist where a 50% score halves the value. After revenue, many angels pay about 3× yearly sales, or match a recently funded competitor. A cash-flow valuation assumes the investor wants at least 25% a year.
  • Valuation gets negotiated down, then tied to targets. On Startup Singam a founder asked ₹2.5 Cr at ₹50 Cr. The investors said they were not convinced by the valuation, cut the round to ₹1.5 Cr, and set the price by a revenue target: ₹30 Cr if turnover reached ₹6 Cr by the date, ₹25 Cr if not, through convertible debentures. The captions are partial, so treat the details as approximate.
  • Fund maths drives everything. Karthik Reddy of Blume on Nikhil Kamath's show: overseas backers of Indian funds expect at least 25% compounded, about five companies return 80–90% of a fund, and term sheets expect an exit in 5–7 years. A company that cannot become one of those five is a hard sell to a VC, whatever the price.
  • Team. On the same panel the investors said a CEO needs a strong technology co-founder beside them, and that building product before understanding the problem usually fails.
  • Most businesses should not raise VC money. Ankur Warikoo: about 90% of businesses are not fundable in the venture sense. Angels usually write ₹5–30 lakh cheques, so ₹1 Cr needs 4–20 of them.

What to correct in the pitch

    What is verified and what is assumed

    • Verified from RBI text and reporting: the 2026 Directions for commercial banks (31 Jul 2026) require source code or an escrow arrangement "or other arrangements", and updates and fixes must be included. Commentary on the parallel NBFC Directions says the same rule sits in the chapter for Middle, Upper and Top Layer NBFCs, not the Base Layer.
    • Verified counts, with a range: 564 Middle Layer and 11 Upper Layer NBFCs at 30 Sep 2025 (FIU-India document). A third-party list shows 628 Middle Layer at 30 Jun 2026. Your figure of about 400 below ₹10,000 Cr is plausible but I could not confirm it from an RBI source.
    • Verified comparable: NCC Group sold Escode, the largest software escrow business, to TDR Capital for £275M enterprise value on £66.5M revenue and £30.9M adjusted EBITDA. That is about 4.1× revenue and 8.9× EBITDA (my arithmetic).
    • Verified, secondary sources: SEBI's CSCRF asks regulated entities to keep SBOMs for critical systems, with CERT-In guidelines as the reference. I found nothing saying an SBOM must be lodged with an escrow agent, so the SBOM add-on here is a product bet, not a mandate.
    • Benchmarks from blogs, not primary data: India pre-seed pre-money ₹3–10 Cr; pre-revenue enterprise SaaS ₹8–18 Cr; private SaaS 3.3–5.3× revenue, 6–8× for fast growers.
    • My assumptions, not sourced: the three share scenarios, churn, SBOM price and attach rate, growth after year 3, the investor's return target, and every Berkus and Scorecard score.
